# FAE Research Notes: Work Order Material Configuration & Port Description ("The unit has three ports")

## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

In industrial automation and field engineering, accurate specification of hardware components, material bills of materials (BOM), and port configurations is vital for work order execution, assembly, and maintenance. 

This research note investigates the linguistic and technical framing of hardware descriptions using the present simple tense (`have`/`has`), specifically focusing on modular industrial units featuring three communication or power ports (e.g., industrial 3-port Ethernet switches, compact edge gateways, or fieldbus I/O modules).

## 4. Linguistic & FAE Technical Communication Matrix

When writing engineering reports, work order instructions, and technical manuals, Field Application Engineers must choose appropriate verb forms to describe permanent physical attributes.

| Semantic Context | Recommended Expression | Alternative / Colloquial | Engineering Usage Guidelines |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Formal Specifications & Manuals** | `The unit has three ports.` | `The unit possesses three ports.` | **Preferred**. Standard present simple tense for permanent physical characteristics and static specifications. |
| **Active Inspection & Assembly** | `The technician has configured three ports.` | `The tech has got three ports set up.` | Use present perfect (`have/has + past participle`) for completed actions during work order execution. |
| **Informal Shop Floor Discussion** | `The module has got three ports.` | `The module has three ports.` | `Have got` is acceptable in verbal communication among engineers but avoided in formal engineering documentation. |
